# Look bible: what the hits look like (from the Commander's references, 2026-11-12)

References in `channel/references/look/`: Lunar Animation "What if the sun exploded?" (2M likes), Then What? "rained for 1,000 years" (1.5M likes), Bolt "never rained again" and "ran out of oil". The 3 `sheet_*.jpg` files show each whole video, 16 frames.

**Key point: the hits are NOT more realistic than us.** They are simple, stylised low-poly. They look better because of art direction.

## The rules
1. **One place, transformed over time.** Most hits keep ONE vantage point for the whole video (a beach from a pier, a lake from a balcony, a freeway from an overpass). Time passes (Day 3 → Day 90 → Year 2) and the same view changes completely. The viewer learns the place in the first frame, so every change reads instantly.
2. **A foreground frame** (balcony railing, fence, window edge) puts the viewer *there* and gives depth.
3. **One consistent style.** Every asset comes from the same low-poly family: cars, trees, people, buildings, props. Never mix cartoon trees with glossy photoreal glass.
4. **Dense, alive, specific.** Hundreds of small details: umbrellas, kites, birds, a Ferris wheel, boats, signs. **Readable story props** ("NO FUEL", "SOLD OUT", "FARM FRESH" truck) tell the story without narration.
5. **Light and atmosphere carry the quality.** Golden-hour or soft daylight, distance haze/fog, a warm or cool colour grade that shifts with the story (blue lake → orange smoke → grey dust). Strong sun, long shadows, bloom on the bright event.
6. **The event fills the frame.** When it hits, it's huge (the blast fills half the sky; dust swallows everything). Never a small dot.
7. **Each caption names one visible change.** "Day 3: the puddles are gone." "Then a church rises out of the lake." The picture shows exactly that.
8. **Slow and long.** 69–84 s, with each beat held 3–6 s and a calm camera (slow drift or locked). The escalation comes from the story, not from fast cuts.
9. **Typography, no boxes:**
   - title: serif, centred, top third, white with a soft shadow;
   - counter: small-caps label ("DAYS WITHOUT RAIN"), then a big number, then a small-caps sub-line, at the left;
   - caption: italic serif, centred, lower middle;
   - **no grey bands or blobs behind text.**
10. **The end card loops.** It's black, with the title again plus one real fact in small text ("Arica, Chile once went 14 years without rain"), and it cuts back to frame 1.
11. **No voice.** Text + music + sound only. Two creators run series: "Post 7", a playlist, labelled AI-generated.

## NEVER SHIP: the checklist (every failure the Commander has caught; check every key frame against it before you call a shot done)
1. **Blobs:** water, dust, smoke or debris made of clumped spheres or lumpy cloud shapes (the "popcorn" geyser, the beige dust blobs, the smooth blue sea wall). Use sheets, droplets, spray, shards, slabs and thin crisp dust ribbons/rings.
2. **Wrong-reading effects:** an implosion that looks like a tornado/twister, layers sliding off each other, a model just scaling down. The viewer must see the OBJECT itself break, buckle and fold.
3. **Light debris for heavy things:** confetti/paper-coloured bits from concrete or steel. Heavy things throw big, dark, heavy chunks that fall under gravity.
4. **Tiny subject in an empty frame:** the event or planet filling less than ~half the frame in its hero moment; a gag subject smaller than a quarter of the frame height.
5. **Bad people:** a person's back filling a big part of the frame, armless or blob-shaped bodies, crowds standing still while the world ends. People need arms, shapes, outfits and big reactions.
6. **Flat shapes:** an untextured triangle mountain, a pale grey flat sky, flat light without long shadows.
7. **Glitch-looking marks:** thin straight lines across the frame, z-fighting, flicker, popping objects.
8. **Fuzzy or mismatched space:** zoomed satellite texture, streaky blur, a photoreal close-up next to a stylised world.
9. **Sameness:** the same towers or plaza as a previous video; the same gag twice.
10. **Text:** bands or boxes behind captions, text over the action, an on-screen DRAMATIZATION tag.
11. **The cause hidden by its own effect:** a splash or dust cloud so big and soft that the object it came from (a ferry, a tower) is gone before the viewer sees what happened to it (the no-empty-space ferry read as a white geyser). Show the object being crushed first, then a modest, crisp splash.
12. **Debris in the lens:** big motion-blurred shards flying at or past the camera so the hero subject disappears for a moment (no-empty-space mountain). Keep debris to the sides and behind; the subject must stay readable in every frame.
13. **A weak last act:** the closing beats (space, scale reveal) shrinking to tiny, still dots. The final comparison must be big and move.

## What this changes for us
- These hits are most likely code-generated, as ours are. No bought assets: build ONE coherent stylised low-poly kit in code (flat-shaded buildings, cars, trees, people and props, all in the same proportions and palette) and use it for every set. Never mix cartoon pieces with glossy photoreal ones.
- Default board shape: one hero vantage, with time steps. Travel to a new place only when the story truly needs it.
- Replace the caption bands with clean shadowed text.
- Longer (60–80 s) and calmer, as the Commander has asked.
